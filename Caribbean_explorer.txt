What do the following columns mean: SA/D | D/L | Ballast %

DETAILED EXPLANATIONS:

---

SA/D RATIO (Sail Area to Displacement)

What It Measures:
The relationship between a boat's sail area and its weight. Specifically: how much "engine" (sail) you have relative to how much weight you're trying to move.

Formula:
SA/D = Sail Area (sq ft) ÷ (Displacement in lbs ÷ 64)^(2/3)

What The Numbers Mean:

| SA/D Ratio | Classification | Characteristics |
|------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Under 15 | Underpowered cruiser | Slow in light air, needs wind to move |
| 15-16 | Heavy cruiser | Comfortable but needs 12+ knots to perform |
| 16-18 | Moderate cruiser | Good all-around performance |
| 18-20 | Performance cruiser | Fast in light-moderate winds |
| 20-25 | Racer-cruiser | Very fast, but may be overpowered in heavy air |
| Over 25 | Racing boat | Too much sail for comfortable cruising |

Caribbean Context:
- Ideal for Caribbean: 16-19
- Trade winds are consistent 15-25 knots
- Higher SA/D (18-20) means faster sailing in typical conditions
- Lower SA/D (14-16) means more comfortable in squalls but slower in light air

Example from our comparison:
- SAGA 409: SA/D 15.45 = Will be slower in light air, comfortable in heavy wind
- Oceanis 40.1: SA/D 19.58 = Fast in typical Caribbean trades, may need reefing sooner in squalls

---

D/L RATIO (Displacement to Length)

What It Measures:
How heavy the boat is relative to its waterline length. This tells you if it's a lightweight flyer or a heavy tank.

Formula:
D/L = (Displacement in lbs ÷ 2240) ÷ (LWL in feet ÷ 100)³

What The Numbers Mean:

| D/L Ratio | Classification | Characteristics |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| Under 100 | Ultra-light racer | Race boats, fragile, uncomfortable |
| 100-150 | Very light | Fast but harsh motion |
| 150-200 | Light | Modern performance cruiser |
| 200-250 | Light-moderate | Good balance of speed and comfort |
| 250-300 | Moderate | Traditional cruiser, comfortable |
| 300-350 | Heavy | Bluewater cruiser, slow but steady |
| Over 350 | Very heavy | Traditional full-keel, maximum comfort |

What This Means for Sailing:

Light Displacement (Under 200):
✓ Faster acceleration
✓ Higher top speeds
✓ More responsive
✗ Harsh, bouncy motion in waves
✗ Slams and pounds in head seas
✗ Less directional stability

Heavy Displacement (Over 300):
✓ Smooth, comfortable motion
✓ Drives through waves instead of over them
✓ Tracks straight with less helm attention
✓ Better self-steering capability
✗ Slower to accelerate
✗ Lower top speed (limited by hull speed)
✗ Needs more wind to get moving

Caribbean Context:
- For comfort in trade wind chop: 250-350 (heavy displacement rides smoother)
- For speed between islands: 180-250 (moderate displacement is faster)
- For shallow draft: Often higher D/L (full keels tend to be heavier)

Example from our comparison:
- SAGA 409: D/L 309 = Heavy cruiser, very comfortable motion at sea, slower but steady
- Oceanis 40.1: D/L 205 = Light-moderate, faster but livelier motion in waves

---

BALLAST RATIO (Ballast/Displacement %)

What It Measures:
What percentage of the boat's total weight is fixed ballast (lead or iron in the keel). Higher = more stability and power to carry sail.

Formula:
Ballast % = (Ballast weight ÷ Total displacement) × 100

What The Numbers Mean:

| Ballast % | Classification | Characteristics |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| Under 30% | Light ballast | Tender, needs early reefing |
| 30-35% | Moderate | Modern cruiser, reasonable stability |
| 35-40% | Good | Stiff boat, carries sail well |
| 40-45% | High | Very stiff, powerful, excellent stability |
| Over 45% | Very high | Race boat or extreme performance |

What This Means for Sailing:

Low Ballast (Under 35%):
✗ Heels more easily
✗ Needs to reef sooner in heavy wind
✗ Less ultimate stability
✓ Lighter displacement = potentially faster in light air
✓ Shallower draft possible

High Ballast (Over 40%):
✓ Stands up to wind better (less heel)
✓ Can carry full sail longer in building winds
✓ More powerful feel
✓ Better ultimate stability (harder to capsize)
✓ Self-rights more easily if knocked down
✗ Heavier = may be slower in light air

Caribbean Context:
- For squall handling: 38-42%+ (high ballast means less panic when dark clouds approach)
- For trade wind power: 35-40% (carries sail well in 20+ knots)
- For modern performance: 30-35% (lighter, faster, but requires more sail management)

Example from our comparison:
- SAGA 409: 40.91% = Very stiff, can carry full sail in 25+ knots, excellent stability
- Oceanis 40.1: 31.40% = Will need to reef sooner, more moderate stability

---

HOW THEY WORK TOGETHER:

Heavy Bluewater Cruiser Example (SAGA 409):
- SA/D 15.45 = Underpowered for light air
- D/L 309 = Heavy displacement = comfortable motion
- Ballast 41% = Very stiff = powerful

Result: Slow in light air but comfortable in heavy weather. Drives through waves. Can carry sail when others are reefed. Perfect for offshore passages in rough conditions.

Modern Performance Cruiser Example (Oceanis 40.1):
- SA/D 19.58 = Well-powered
- D/L 205 = Light-moderate displacement = faster
- Ballast 31% = Moderate stability

Result: Fast in light-moderate winds. Quicker between islands. More lively motion in waves. Needs to reef earlier in squalls. Better for coastal cruising.
